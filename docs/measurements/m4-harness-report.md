## What this does and does not establish

Item by item, because a single disclaimer at the bottom is a disclaimer a reader skips.

The unverified rows cover every item on `PLAN.md` §8's list. That list has nine entries and this table has 8: §8 names the camera-to-process delay twice — once as `SENSOR_TRANSPORT_LAG_S` and once as "RTSP 端到端延遲" — and they are the same quantity.

| Claim | Status | |
|---|---|---|
| Distance readings lag reality, and by how much | simulated | p95 43ms, against a synthetic camera at 30 fps with the real detector in the loop. |
| The frame buffer has no backpressure | simulated | It stayed shallow here, but nothing bounds it: a consumer slower than the camera fills it without limit, which tests/test_diagnostics.py demonstrates on purpose. |
| The approach controller converges under a stale reading, up to a point | simulated | converges up to 1.55s of transport lag; at 1.60s it fails by overshoot, and breaches the safety floor from 1.75s (located to ±0.05s by the sweep grid) [parameter sweep, NOT a measurement — sensor_transport_lag_s has never been measured and cannot be without hardware] |
| The controller reports success even when it has failed | simulated | Every row of the sweep returns `arrived`, including rows that end inside the safety floor. It judges arrival from the same stale reading it steers by. |
| Camera-to-process delay on a real robot (segment A) | **UNVERIFIED** | Exposure, on-robot encoding and RTSP over WiFi are upstream of where the harness injects frames. Swept, never measured. This is `PLAN.md` §8's `SENSOR_TRANSPORT_LAG_S` and its "RTSP 端到端延遲" — one quantity, listed twice. *Would be settled by: Timestamping a frame at the robot and again on arrival, which needs a Misty II on a network.* |
| Travel speed at the commanded drive percentage | **UNVERIFIED** | `cm_per_sec_at_percent` is an assumption. The sweep shows a robot travelling twice as far as commanded walks through the safety floor. *Would be settled by: drive_time(linearVelocity=20, timeMs=2000) on a real robot, then measuring the distance covered.* |
| Camera focal constant | **UNVERIFIED** | `focal_length` sets the distance estimate's scale. Every centimetre figure here is relative to it. *Would be settled by: Standing at a measured 100cm and scaling by actual / reported.* |
| The driver layer's HTTP and WebSocket requests are accepted | **UNVERIFIED** | M3's contract tests prove the request format matches docs.mistyrobotics.com. They cannot prove the robot obeys. *Would be settled by: Sending them to a Misty II and observing the response.* |
| The motors turn at all at the commanded percentage | **UNVERIFIED** | `drive_percent` is 20% of maximum. Whether that clears the motor deadband is unknown; the robot may simply not move. *Would be settled by: Issuing a drive at 20% and watching whether anything happens.* |
| Speech is recognised through Misty's microphone | **UNVERIFIED** | The ASR adapter is tested against its own interface. Recognition rate against Misty's microphone in a room with noise in it is untouched. *Would be settled by: Speaking to a Misty II and reading the transcripts.* |
| What happens when a drive command arrives mid-motion | **UNVERIFIED** | The approach loop waits `post_step_settle_s` and assumes the previous motion finished. Misty's actual behaviour if it has not is undocumented and untested. *Would be settled by: Issuing overlapping drive_time calls on a real robot.* |
| The robot ever approached anyone | **UNVERIFIED** | `approach_user()` has never executed on hardware. The public demo the project came from produced `movement: "stay"`. *Would be settled by: Running an episode on a Misty II.* |

## Why half the path cannot be measured

```
[truth] ─ exposure → encode → RTSP over WiFi ─▶ [in process] ─ buffer/detect ─▶ [reading]
        └──────── segment A: needs hardware ───┘  └──────── segment B: measured here ────┘
```

The harness injects frames exactly where RTSP would deliver them, so segment B is covered end to end with the real detector running on real pixels. Segment A is upstream of that point and needs a robot on a network to observe at all.

It is therefore **swept, not measured**: the report above says how large it would have to be before the controller stops converging, which is the strongest statement available without hardware. It is not a claim about how large it actually is.

This is the same line M3 drew around the driver layer: the contract tests prove the requests match the published API, and prove nothing about whether the robot obeys them.
